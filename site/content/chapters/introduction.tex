%% title: Agreement as a local phenomenon

\label{ch:intro}

When two people are talking, words exit the mouth of one, and enter the ears of the other. Neither can see anything happening between them, so the interaction seems \textbf{long-distance}. 

Underlyingly, the speaker’s mouth is pushing the air particles next to it, and those air particles are pushing the air particles next to them, and those air particles are pushing the air particles next to \textit{them}, until finally some air particles push against the listener’s eardrum. What seems long-distance is actually built from many smaller \textbf{local} interactions.

In syntax, one of the most well-known long-distance phenomena is agreement. In fact, some authors propose that \textit{all} long-distance phenomena in syntax can be reduced to agreement \citep{deal2025}. In this squib, I demonstrate that some of the most prominent formulations of agreement in Minimalism – variations on Chomsky’s \citeyearpar{chomsky2001} \textbf{Probe-Goal Agree} – can be reformalized as local theories, where seemingly long-distance patterns result from many smaller local interactions.
