% Summary of boeckx2009: On long-distance {Agree}

\begin{abstract}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Few on-the-surface LDA patterns are actually long-distance, but some are.
  \item Even when LDA patterns are long-distance, Agree cannot cross phases.
\end{enumerate}

Key empirical facts:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item In Hindi-Urdu, (in addition to what Bhatt 2005 says) agreeing embedded objects can scope over matrix predicates, but not non-agreeing ones.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Chandra 2007 argues this means the object moves and does Spec-Head Agreement, but it could be Covert Movement or agreement could enlarge scope domains.
  \end{itemize}
  \item In German and Itelmen, the agreeing element \textit{must} take wide scope over matrix material.
  \item However, in Tsez, the agreeing element can't scope out.
  \item Basque is crazy and I need to read the section on it again.
  \item Icelandic only allows nominatives to trigger agreement, and agreeing nominative objects (with quirky subjects) only trigger 3rd-person agreement. Agreement with nominative objects is obligatory mono-clausally, and optional across clausal boundaries. Some other data I need to look back at.
  \item Polinsky 2003 proposes that surface LDA could be prolepsis (as in Blackfoot and Fox), raising like ECM (as in Quechua and Kipsigis), restructuring (as in German), or true Agree from the edge of the embedded clause (Tsez).
  \item Also mentioned but didn't discuss in depth Passamaquoddy and Innu-Aimun, which could be analyzed as having either local or long-distance agreement (Bruening 2001, Branigan and MacKenzie 2002).
\end{enumerate}
\end{abstract}
