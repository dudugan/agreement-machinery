% Summary of bhatt2005: Long distance agreement in {Hindi-Urdu}

\begin{abstract}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item In Hindi-Urdu, verbs agree with the highest non-overtly case-marked argument.
  \item When there's none of these in the matrix clause, and there's an embedded clause under a restructuring verb, and there's an absolutive object there, verbs agree with the absolutive object.
  \item This points to a dissociation between case and agreement, and a relegation of the Activity Condition to Move rather than Agree
\end{enumerate}
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Vivek-ERG [book.fs read-INF.fs] want-PFV.fs
\end{enumerate}

Sometimes optional; people say the object in (2) sounds more `specific' than it does in (3):

\begin{enumerate}[start=2]
  \item Ram-ERG [bread.fs eat-INF.fs] want-PFV.fs
  \item Ram-ERG [bread.fs eat-INF.ms] want-PFV.ms
\end{enumerate}

In Hindi-Urdu, the agreement of the matrix verb and the embedded infinitival are always identical.

LDA is also banned when with finite embedded clauses, gerunds, infinitival clauses with case, or subject infinitivals - aka, all the environments where restructuring is banned.
